A point from every angle

Read it. Call it. Keep it.

01 / PLAYBOOK

A call every player can read.

Pick the field and the break, or write your own. Route, face, shot lane and job for each of the five, in your team's own words. Switch ends and it turns round with you.

02 / TALLY

Tap the bunker. Tap who won it.

Chart the break bunker by bunker. Set your format once and the sheet calls match point, overtime or a tie. By Sunday it tells you which bunkers win you points on this field.

03 / SCOUT

Remember the other pit.

Their five by name, the calls you have seen them run and your answer to each, kept against the team. Watch a game you are not in and scout both benches.

04 / SIGHTLINES

What a bunker can see.

Ask from any bunker. Drag the lane to the spot you actually shoot at and see what is in the way, in feet.

Scout reads and sightlines are coaching aids. They are not predictions or a substitute for the officials' live call.

One day at an event

Calm on the sideline.

Two minutes between points, and every tap saves time instead of costing it. Look down and the answer is there: the call, the score, who's on, what they ran last time. Look up and coach.

  1. 01 / THE NIGHT BEFORE

    Walk onto the field already knowing it.

    The event's field is already there, measured off the official map. Pick the breaks you run or tap your own onto the bunkers, and every player gets a card with his bunker, his lane and his job in your team's words.

  2. 02 / THE FIELD WALK

    Tap a bunker. See what it sees.

    Your notes stand beside the bunker like sticky notes. Ask what can be seen from there and the lanes draw themselves, clear or blocked, in feet.

  3. 03 / BETWEEN POINTS

    Score it, call it, set the five.

    One tap for who won it. The break clock counts itself down. Two taps to swap a man. The next call sits under the score, with what they ran last time in this spot and how often.

  4. 04 / OFF THE BUZZER

    Tap the bunker. That's the log.

    Tap where each man went. Wrong bunker? Undo. No forms, no typing, no lists while the point is live.

  5. 05 / THE NEXT OPPONENT

    Scout them from the fence.

    Pick the game off the schedule and tap Watch this game. Chart both teams; none of it touches your own record.

  6. 06 / THE DRIVE HOME

    What you learned Saturday is waiting Sunday.

    Your record at the event, the bunkers that win you points, the calls that were read, and your answer to each team's favourite break. Then the drill: two seconds to name a break from the field alone.

How it looks, how it holds up, how it handles

Built for one hand and two minutes.

The look.

Stadium dark: black ground, red light, off-white type that reads under lights and in full sun. The real layout for every event, bunkers in the colours the map paints them. Each pit its own wire colour, every shot lane white, so a glance is enough. Thumb-sized buttons, and on an iPad the field and the sheet stand side by side.

The quality.

Bunkers measured off the event's own map, not guessed. Every number is counted from what you logged, never invented, and when there is not enough logged yet it says so. Wrong bunker, wrong man, wrong score: one tap undoes it.

The ease.

The score, the call and the five live on one sheet. Name the breaks, the bunkers and the jobs the way your team shouts them. A ? on every screen answers the question for that screen. Answer the format once and every match starts with it.

The field has no bars.

Gridlock does not need them. It works with no signal, and your season stays on your phone, where nobody else reads your scouting. Keep a copy and carry it to the next phone.

Between points

The two minutes that decide the next one.

The score, one tap.

We won it or they won it. The score rides in the header on every screen. Race to 4, 5, 7, or one point a game for 5-man: the sheet calls match point and tells you when it is over.

The clock you run.

Set the break clock to your event's rule. It starts itself when a point is scored and counts down on every screen, so the five are set before the buzzer.

Who's on.

Nine men, five slots. Tap one off, tap one on. The five stands until you change it, and Playbook and the cards name them.

The horn.

When the official's clock runs out you say what your format does: ahead, the match ends; level, a tie or next point wins. Penalties are recorded as what they cost, never called.

Between events

What you learned, kept.

The record.

Won, lost, tied and open at this event, counted from finished sheets. Every sheet you have kept, with how it ended.

Cards.

One card a man: his bunker, his job, which way he faces, where he lanes. Hand them over or share the five.

Rep.

The recognition drill. The field draws a break with nothing naming it, and a two-second clock holds you to the call.

Counter.

Your answer to each of their calls, written down against the team and shown beside the call on game day.
