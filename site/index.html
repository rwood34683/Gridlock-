Playbook

Between points you get about forty seconds.

Playbook is built around the four things a coach actually does in that window — in that order, on one screen.

  1. 1

    Pick the event

    Choose the field and the layout loads everywhere — Playbook, Scout, Sightlines, the walk, the stats.

  2. 2

    Pick the break

    Hold & Read, Balanced Break, Snake Stack, Dorito Flood, Blitz. Each one runs its own five paths.

  3. 3

    Direct the five

    Every player gets a job, a primary and a secondary. Turn on Face and Shot lanes to show where they point.

  4. 4

    Log the break

    Mark what you called and who went out. It stays with the team for the next point.

The field

A real layout, and it does the geometry for you.

Bunkers sit where they sit — doritos, cans, temples, wedges, the snake, the beam — with the official codes printed on them. Dorito side up, snake side down, the way you look at it from the box.

  • Five pathsEach player leaves the start line on his own line and plants on his own bunker. Staggered off the buzzer — the snake runner first, support behind.
  • FacePoint a player any of eight ways and the chevron travels with him down the path, so you see where he is looking at every step, not just where he ends up.
  • Shot lanesGive a player a bunker to lane and the cone follows him, with a ring on the target. Leave it unset and he lanes the way he is running.
  • SightlinesPick the bunker you are in and every lane to every other bunker is drawn and tested. Green clears. Red clips something on the way. Real geometry, not a sketch.
  • Real layoutsThe NXL 2026 Midwest Open ships digitized from the official labeled 2D — all 58 bunkers measured off the printed 10-ft grid, not sketched.
  • Switch the eventChange the layout once and every tab redraws on the new field, breakout paths included.

A training aid, not a prediction. Sightlines are geometry — they do not know where bodies are. Officials govern the live call.

Scout

Read the other pit before the buzzer.

Two opponent cards, left pit and right pit. Set the tendency you saw on film, star the threat, and keep your notes on the snake runner where you will actually find them.

Left pit Right pit Both breaks draw on the same field.
  • MatchupA sim bar, the wire split — snake front, snake, centre-50, dorito — and who goes where for all ten players.
  • AnticipatePer team: what they are likely to run, the tells at the line, and your counter.
  • Counter-pickerRanks all five breaks against the tendency you logged and the state of the match. Patient calls top the list when you are up; aggressive ones when you have to score. Tap the top row and it becomes the call.
  • Division boardThe whole division in one table — registration, film, tendency, threat. Tap a row to load it into the right pit.

Heuristic reads are a training aid, not a prediction. The percentages come from your own film reads and seeded data. Officials govern the live call.

Clinics and league ops

For the people running the day.

Clinics

Staff create a class and get a join code. Share the code, players enter it, and their sign-in form lands under that class.

GL-7K2M

Joining does not need an account. A player types the code, fills in name, experience and preferred wire, and they are on your list. Only creating the class needs a staff login.

League blasts

Admins keep the event's groups and send one update to the people who need it.

Ops Refs Registration Vendors
  • Pick one group or several, write the update, send.
  • Goes out by push and email, or hands off to your phone's Messages with the numbers filled in.
  • Every blast is logged — body, groups, recipient count, time and who sent it.

Not a bulk-messaging tool. Only message people who agreed to league ops texts.
